# Complete Analysis and Demonstration Guide — CSE570 Bank Marketing Project

This guide is designed for a live project demonstration, report writing and viva.
Run the script first, then use this guide to explain every stage. Do not invent
findings: copy numerical values from your own `outputs/` files and explain the
actual plots generated by your run.

## A. Project objective and prediction-time definition

**Business question:** Using information available before a marketing call, can we
estimate whether a customer will subscribe to a term deposit?

**Target:** `y` (`yes` = 1, `no` = 0). This is binary classification.

**Prediction-time rule:** `duration` is excluded from the primary model because it
is the length of the current call and is not available before that call. Including
it would answer a different, post-call question and leak information unavailable
at the intended decision time.

**Intended user:** A campaign analyst who prioritizes outreach for review. The
model must not be presented as a causal tool or as a reason to deny services.

## B. Step-by-step analysis: what to show and what to explain

### Step 1 — Dataset provenance and loading
**Show:** UCI source page, `data_profile.json`, first rows printed in terminal.

**Explain:** The dataset is the UCI Bank Marketing `bank-full` release. The full
file has 45,211 records, 16 predictors and one target (`y`), i.e. 17 columns.
The profile file records the exact shape actually downloaded.

**Analysis to write:** State the row/column counts from your profile. Explain why
the dataset suits a supervised binary classification task: it has a labelled
subscription outcome, mixed numeric/categorical predictors and a documented
public source.

### Step 2 — Data quality and cleaning
**Show:** `missing_value_summary.csv`, `data_profile.json`, `data_dictionary_profile.csv`.

**Explain each check:**
- Duplicate rows are counted; exact duplicates are removed.
- Actual NaN values are summarized. Numeric NaNs are median-imputed and
  categorical NaNs are most-frequent-imputed inside the training pipeline.
- Literal `unknown` category values are counted separately. They are not silently
  converted to NaN or deleted because they are recorded category values.
- Data types and unique counts are profiled.
- `pdays = -1` is a documented sentinel for no previous contact; do not treat it
  as an ordinary positive number of days without explaining its meaning.

**Outlier analysis:** Review `outlier_*_boxplot.png` and distribution plots.
Do not automatically remove extreme balances or contact counts: they may be
valid customer/campaign observations. State which variables show skew/extreme
values, and justify retaining them unless you have evidence they are errors.

**Analysis to write:** Give duplicate count, missingness, unknown counts and
outlier observations from your own run. Distinguish missing values from
`unknown` categories.

### Step 3 — Target distribution and class imbalance
**Show:** `01_target_distribution.png`, `data_profile.json`.

**Explain:** Count and percentage of `yes` and `no`. The classes are imbalanced:
the `no` group is larger. This means a classifier can obtain deceptively high
accuracy by predicting the majority class.

**Analysis to write:** Report the actual counts and percentages. Explain why
stratified splitting and precision, recall, F1 and PR-AUC are appropriate.

**Explain:** A univariate analysis studies one variable at a time. Compare shape,

**Analysis to write:** For each plotted variable, describe what the actual

**Explain:** The correlation heatmap summarizes pairwise linear correlations

**Analysis to write:** Name the strongest positive/negative correlations visible

### Step 7 — Statistical analysis
**Show:** `statistical_tests.json`.

**Test 1: Chi-square test of independence**
- Question: Is job category associated with subscription outcome?
- Null hypothesis H0: job and subscription are independent.
- Alternative H1: there is an association.
- Report chi-square statistic, degrees of freedom, p-value and Cramér's V.
- If p < 0.05, describe evidence of association under the test assumptions.
- Cramér's V describes association strength; do not rely only on p-value.

**Test 2: Mann–Whitney U**
- Question: Do balance values differ in distribution between the yes and no groups?
- H0: the two groups have the same distribution (interpret carefully).
- H1: their distributions differ.
- Report U statistic, p-value and group medians.
- The test does not require normally distributed data, but observations should
  be independent.

**Important:** Statistical significance is not causality. Large datasets can
produce small p-values for small effects. Discuss effect size and practical meaning.

### Step 8 — Train/test split and leakage prevention
**Show:** `train.py` sections for `train_test_split`, `ColumnTransformer` and
`Pipeline`.

**Explain:**
- 80% training and 20% test, stratified by target, with fixed random seed.
- Test data remains untouched during tuning and model selection.
- Numeric missing values are median-imputed and scaled.
- Categorical missing values are most-frequent-imputed and one-hot encoded.
- All preprocessing is inside a scikit-learn Pipeline, so each CV fold learns
  transformations only from its training portion.
- `duration` is excluded from the pre-call prediction feature set.

### Step 9 — Baseline and model development
**Show:** `model_comparison_cv.csv` and the model definitions in `train.py`.

**Models:**
1. DummyClassifier — majority-class baseline.
2. Logistic Regression — linear decision boundary; class weighting helps address
   imbalance.
3. Decision Tree — nonlinear, rule-based model; can overfit without constraints.
4. Random Forest — ensemble of trees; reduces variance through averaging.
5. HistGradientBoosting — boosting model that sequentially improves predictions.

**Explain:** The baseline establishes a minimum reference. The other four are
predictive ML models. The same training split, preprocessing strategy and CV
folds are used for a fair comparison.

### Step 10 — Cross-validation and hyperparameter tuning
**Show:** `GridSearchCV` and `StratifiedKFold` in `train.py`, plus
`model_comparison_cv.csv`.

**Explain:** Five-fold stratified CV repeatedly trains on four folds and validates
on the remaining fold, preserving approximate class proportions. GridSearchCV
tests specified hyperparameter combinations. The selection criterion is mean
cross-validated F1 among non-baseline models.

**Caution:** The compact grids are a practical assignment-sized search, not an
exhaustive optimization of every possible parameter.

### Step 11 — Final held-out evaluation
**Show:** `final_test_metrics.json`, `03_confusion_matrix.png`,
`04_roc_curve.png`, `05_precision_recall_curve.png`.

**Explain metrics:**
- Accuracy: proportion of all correct predictions.
- Precision: among predicted subscribers, proportion actually subscribing.
- Recall: among actual subscribers, proportion identified.
- F1: harmonic mean of precision and recall.
- ROC-AUC: ranking discrimination across thresholds.
- PR-AUC / average precision: summarizes precision-recall performance, useful
  when the positive class is uncommon.
- Confusion matrix: true negatives, false positives, false negatives, true positives.

**Business interpretation:** False positives may lead to outreach to customers
who do not subscribe; false negatives may miss potential subscribers. The
appropriate trade-off depends on campaign costs and capacity.

**Analysis to write:** Insert the actual test metrics and confusion matrix. Explain
which error type matters more to the bank and why. Do not claim a score before
running the experiment.

### Step 12 — Feature interpretation
**Show:** `permutation_importance.csv` and `06_permutation_importance.png`.

**Explain:** A feature is shuffled in the held-out data and the decrease in F1 is
measured. Larger positive decreases indicate greater reliance by this fitted
model. Importance is model- and dataset-dependent, can be affected by correlated
features, and is not a causal effect.

### Step 13 — Conclusion and limitations
Conclude using actual observed results. Discuss:
- best model selected using training-only CV F1;
- its held-out metrics;
- most important features;
- class imbalance and threshold dependence;
- institution/time-specific historical data;
- unknown category values;
- possible temporal drift and need for external validation;
- no causal inference and no guarantee of campaign uplift.
